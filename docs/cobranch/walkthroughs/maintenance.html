Workload · cobranch maintenance

maintenance: the hourly sweep

A CronJob that deletes what expired and keeps the object store tidy. It is the only housekeeping that runs on a clock rather than on a queue, and it reuses the services the servers use rather than SQL of its own.

Runs asCronJob, 0 * * * *, deadline 50 minutes so it never skips the next hour
DiskAn emptyDir (16Gi by default) for repacking: above twice the largest repository's packs
Wiringcmd/cobranch/maintenance.go, 110 lines, the whole workload

Acts asNobody: its changes carry no principal in the audit log

Things to know

What it relies on

  • Compaction needs a store it can reach. With a file:// store on each Git server pod's own emptyDir, maintenance cannot see the packs, and the chart leaves compaction off.
  • The git-server tolerates it mid-fetch. A fetch works from one snapshot and hard-links its packs, so a pack superseded during the fetch stays readable; deletion waits a day.
  • Errors are joined, not short-circuited. errors.Join across the steps means one failing step still lets the others run, and the Job fails at the end.
  • The controller sweeps too. Pods, run tokens, schedule slots and renders are cleaned by the controller's own Sweep, on its interval.

Worth a closer look

Two homes for expiry

Rows expire here, hourly; tokens, slots and renders expire in the controller's sweeps. One rule for where "delete what is past its time" lives would make the next table's home obvious.

A full service to call one method

deleteExpired builds an auth.Service, an orgs.Service and an authorizer to reach three delete queries. A small expiry port would need none of them.
